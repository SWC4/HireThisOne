The holidays have come and gone, it's time to keep things rolling along. I have some posts to put up and projects to start, but today, we're going to look at just one little tool in this whole mess of technology we're all working with. I'm best described as someone who's cautious about the presence of use of AI, [particularly because the companies involved has some financial concerns](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/big-tech/openai-could-reportedly-run-out-of-cash-by-mid-2027-nyt-analyst-paints-grim-picture-after-examining-companys-finances). I think new technology should be adopted carefully and with some caution, especially in a large organization.

# How I Used an AI

This whole website exists because of my search for a new job. The market right now is a bit rough. I've applied to various positions with different variations of my resume. I watch the analytics embedded in my files and see where interest develops. I'm almost A/B testing my resume  against different clients to see what will eventually get a job offer. My job search is like anything else: a problem to solve. Data, time, and research can solve any problem, even a job hunt in a tough market.

Over the last few months I have tried:

- Existing, old resumes from prior job searches
- Various resume builder tools
- AI-Tailored resumes

The results are pretty clear: I'm still unemployed.

Recruiters posting on LinkedIn have thrown out all manner of advice, both good and bad. There's common threads and complaints, many angry at "fake" resumes, AI Generated resumes, resumes with AI hallucinations, and more of the same. There's a demand for "authenticity." Others want a clear "story" about the job applicant, better positioning, something that makes it clear who's good for the role. There's also the demand for "metrics" about prior roles. At the same time, "conventional" wisdom calls for a resume no longer than 2 pages, and made primarily of bullet points so it can easily be skimmed.

All these resumes look the same, the space constraint removes information, and I think they're ineffective.

## Making a Better Resume

My solution to these problems has been to design a new Resume Layout in LaTeX. It's something you can't make in any of the major online tools, it's not something you'd make in Word, and it conveys information I think more clearly and quickly in 5 pages than a 2 page resume. The problem is finding out if any of it is  good or worthwhile before I actually start relying on it.

I asked Claude. And Gemini.

![An output from Claude assessing me as an ideal candidate](@blog/Claude-opinion-1.png)

I gathered some publicly available resumes from other technical writers, fed them into the AI with some  job descriptions, and I let the AI pick who it liked best. In principal, the AI doesn't know who I am or my connection to the resumes, so it should be relatively impartial. The majority of the time, the AI's pick mine for the job postings. That doesn't mean a human will, but it is a validation that I'm on a plausible right-path.

I'm using these AIs to root out flaws in my resume design and hopefully better targeting the ideal jobs.

## My Future Use of AI

I think it's interesting how it works and what it does. The results are interesting. I think part of the problem we'll have to face is that AI's as they exist right now are an amplifier. The technology does more to boost what you're doing than anything else. If you're working on a bad day, all the AI wants to do is amplify that bad idea. I was able to constantly be the devil's advocate and the AI would always change its mind. In that sense, it can't make a solid decision or offer necessarily concrete guidance.

In my case, I think it's helpful to receive positive words from the AI, but I can't say the AI is actually picking the best candidate. If I can ask it a question and it changes its guidance so readily, I wonder if other factors are driving its output.

# The Future of AI as a Whole

I think there are places an AI could be interesting, but I also still think the market for it will collapse. The best use of it, I think still has to be targeted. It's going to at-most be a service with low to moderate demand, best served by some niche companies. Claude Code could be one that survives or maybe Google's Gemini will survive as the ultimate assistant.

At the end of the day, for any of this to be useful and survive: the finances have to work. Maybe there's some potential here, something useful, but I don't think it's multi-trillion-dollar and bail-out-OpenAI scales of useful.
