The blog went a little quiet there, didn't it? My github activity from August to December was straight updates, building this site, and then it all went quiet. I might've taken an extended Christmas/New Year's break. Months of job applications going nowhere, bad news coming at a constant flow, and I just needed a break. That doesn't mean I stopped doing things though. Oh no, I was busier than ever, just in different places.

This is just an up date about everything. No job-market-blues here. Just fun stuff.

# Side Projects

I spread my focus to things that didn't involve my site, my job search, or the awful state of our economy. At least, to the best of my ability. Would it surprise you to hear it's still been coding and building things?

## The LARP Site

I have a friend who has been trying to build a Live Action Role Play group. If only there were someone with a ton of experience web development and marketing. If only we had a tool that could turn static content like markdown files into something useful and presentable as a website. After that, it'd just be a matter of moving some text files and the site wouldj ust build itself, right? If only..

Oh yes, Astro my beloved static generator, you'll save the day again.

The plan at first was that I would just set up the coding bits and the LARP crew would write up the site content. They would write the rules page, the event page, everything. All I had to do was teach them to use Obsidian and mind my own business until they sent me something. A few months ticked by and unfortunately nobody wanted to learn Obsidian. Or Markdown. What I got was generally copy/pasted text from the google docs rule book they'd made. It wasn't formatted and it needed a lot of work. 

It was time to make some content.

I spent about 2 months rewriting everything, expanding content, squeezing information from the group, and updating the design of the site. We started with DockKit for a template and I tweaked it into a perfect system. We explain the rules, the character options, the upcoming event, and it even has a built in webstore via Paypal's horrendous checkout button (it broke the site's CSS and caused me too many issues to ever use it again).

At some point I might do some posts here and link to that site, but not today. I'm still working on it.

## The Woodshop

If you're a creator, you'll create with anything. The need to make things is undeniable. You're going to write, code, paint,  something. Instead of losing my mind spending 10 hour days putting in job applications that I'll never hear back on, I got a Ryobi toolkit, a workbench, and some other bits to transform my garage into a workshop. My grandpa had a workshop that he used for making bird houses, bird feeders, wind chimes, and even used to build a chicken coup in his 70s. I've been working on being precise, building furniture, and other small items. I have a long way to go, but I'm going to have a good little shop.

If I get good enough at what I do, maybe I'll pivot from animation and into the simpler life of both building and selling furniture. 

My next plans are to build some massive shelves and another workstation, this one dedicated to my miter saw for precise cuts. 


# The Effect of Escaping

Moving away from my job search a bit has helped tremendously. I'm sending out fewer job apps, but I'm feeling better overall. I'm angry at our economy. I'm angry at employers who never respond. I'm angry for the jobs I know I can do but never hear back on. That's natural, I think. My time away has at least given me days and hours where I'm not angry at things (except perhaps the hammer that caught my thumb).

It's important to take a break.


# The Future

I'm still job and client hunting. I'm starting to expand my hunt in tech-writing to freelance writing opportunities and freelance content writing of any type. A big part of me wants to overhaul this site. I want to add sections for other writing samples, writing guides, and maybe a series of employer-targeted landing pages.
